The useful moment is before the feed.

Appause does not decide which apps are bad or lock you out. It makes an automatic opening noticeable while the choice still matters.

  1. 01

    Notice the opening

    The pause appears as a selected app comes to the foreground, before the habit takes over.

  2. 02

    Name the reason

    A simple prompt turns “just checking” into a reason you can see and reconsider.

  3. 03

    Make the call

    Continue when it is intentional, or cancel and return home. The decision stays yours.

A guided tour of your boundary.

The interface follows one continuous story: choose where you want friction, then learn what happened when it appeared.

01 / Set the boundary

Start with the context, not a blacklist.

Group apps that pull you into the same habit, choose a cooldown, and keep a better next action visible when you need it.

02 / Learn from the result

See choices, not surveillance.

Statistics stay on your device and show completed or avoided openings, helping you notice patterns without creating an account.

Completed
You continued after making the opening explicit.
Avoided
You chose not to enter the selected app.
Local history

Powerful enough to interrupt. Limited enough to trust.

Appause explains each permission by the job it performs. Core detection uses app identity, not the content on your screen.

Required

Accessibility service

Detects which app comes to the foreground and shows the Appause intervention. canRetrieveWindowContent=false, so Appause does not retrieve screen content or read your input.

Recommended

Usage Access

An optional compatibility and confirmation layer that also supports usage statistics. The core pause flow uses Accessibility.

Device fallback

Display over other apps

The preferred accessibility overlay path does not need this separate permission. Some device-specific fallback paths may require it.

Local-first by design. Normal use stays on your phone. There is no account or background analytics; network access is limited to actions you initiate, such as downloads or opening support links.
